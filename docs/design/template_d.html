Akinwande Oluwole Soyinka
School No. #482 1946 Set First Black African Nobel Prize Winner
Profile
Nobel Prize in Literature (1986) Pyrates Confraternity Founder National Arts Theatre Namesake University of Leeds Alumni Political Prisoner 1967–1969

Akinwande Oluwole Babatunde Soyinka was born on 13 July 1934 in Aké, Abeokuta. A child prodigy and avid reader, his parents encouraged his literary pursuits from an early age. By 1945, aged only 11, he gained admission into Abeokuta Grammar School. His ever-inquisitive spirit led him to transfer within one year to the more prestigious Government College Ibadan in 1946.

During his years in GCI, Soyinka did well in his studies but was rebellious and difficult to deal with. He spent time in all 4 Houses. Grier House was the last house he called home at GCI, qualifying him as a proud Grierson.

After five years in GCI, he enrolled in 1952 at the University of Ibadan. Ever a rebel and iconoclast, he founded, while an undergraduate, the Pyrates Confraternity — to “bring back the age of chivalry and fight for social justice.” He left Nigeria in 1954 for the University of Leeds, where his literary creativity truly blossomed.

“The man dies in all who keep silent in the face of tyranny.”

Wole Soyinka  ·  The Man Died, 1972

On his return to Nigeria in 1959, Soyinka worked as a lecturer in the Drama Department at the Universities of Ife, Lagos and Ibadan. After Independence in 1960, he became alarmed at Nigeria’s political direction. For his conscience-driven opposition to the Civil War and his social activism, he was jailed for 22 months from August 1967 to October 1969. His prison experience is told in his famous book, The Man Died.

In 1986, Soyinka was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature, the first black African to be so honoured. The citation referred to his “wide cultural perspective and poetic overtones fashioning the drama of existence.” In July 2024, President Bola Tinubu named the National Arts Theatre after him to commemorate his 90th birthday.

Throughout his life, Soyinka fought against oppression wherever he saw it. Today, we celebrate him as the most famous writer and poet that Nigeria has ever produced: a proud Grierson and a global icon for human rights the world over.
